# Cohort A: US/Western self-made tech founders and operators

Research date: 2026-11-20. Compiled for the Second Brain accountability mentor.

## METHOD AND HONEST LIMITS (read first)

- Tools: WebSearch (summaries of result pages) plus WebFetch. Only two pages were read in full by me via WebFetch (CBS excerpt of Gates's *Source Code*, CNBC copy of Bezos's 2017 shareholder letter); several other fetches (GeekWire, Stanford news) returned HTTP 403. Everything else rests on search-engine summaries of the cited URLs, not on my reading the full page. So "high" confidence below means: multiple independent outlets agree or it is the person's own primary text. Nothing is "high" just because it is famous.
- Searches were done per person, with queries that asked about failures, habits, mentors. Where a search returned nothing for a field, the field says "not found". I did not fill gaps from memory.
- Not researched at all (despite being on the brief): Paul Allen (appears only inside Gates's entry), Sam Altman's OpenAI firing, Eric Schmidt beyond Bill Campbell material. Page and Brin share one entry (thin).
- Entries counted: N = 37 entries (38 people, Page + Brin combined). Depth is uneven: roughly 15 entries have 3+ supported fields; the rest are thin (marked). Wealth figures are only given when a source gave them.
- Large caveat: many sources are secondhand listicle/news aggregators (Business Insider, Inc., Fortune recaps, Shortform, Medium, Stacksync, Rework). Primary sources used: Stanford's Jobs speech page/transcripts, Bezos shareholder letter (CNBC copy), Gates memoir excerpt (CBS), Y Combinator blog posts, Tim Ferriss transcripts (listed, not read in full).

## Bill Gates (partly: family background not researched; software; one of the richest people, exact figure not sourced)
- Early years and failures: Memoir *Source Code* excerpt: at Lakeside he wrote tic-tac-toe programs that "failed all the time"; debugging meant logging off and re-queueing for costly computer time, so he wrote programs on paper first. Admitted he was driven by a need to prove himself and not to be bested by older boys. Dropped out of Harvard to build Microsoft. A Traf-O-Data failure was NOT confirmed in my sources.
- Habits: Worked weekends, skipped vacations in early Microsoft years ("quite fanatical about work", BBC 2016 per Fox/Inc summaries). Tracked who left the parking lot early/late (CNBC 2018). Reads about 50 books a year, takes margin notes, blocks at least an hour for reading (Time, Founding Fuel, Inc.; secondary). "Think Week" frequency not verified.
- Mentors/accountability: Paul Allen as co-founder (partner, not coach). Buffett friendship: not found in my results.
- Avoidance/comfort: Admitted the early intensity was "not always appropriate" once he became a father (2023 NAU speech via Inc/Fortune).
- Setbacks: Not found in detail.
- Quote: "fanatical about work" (via secondary summaries).
- Sources: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/book-excerpt-source-code-my-beginnings-by-bill-gates ; https://www.foxbusiness.com/business-leaders/bill-gates-explains-weekends-vacations-microsoft ; https://www.cnbc.com/2018/04/25/bill-gates-memorized-microsoft-employees-license-plates-to-track-them.html ; https://time.com/4786837/bill-gates-books-reading/ ; Confidence: medium (primary excerpt is high but narrow).

## Steve Jobs (partly/yes; hardware/software; scale not sourced)
- Early/failure: Fired from Apple in 1985 after Macintosh launch; in the Stanford 2005 speech he called it ultimately beneficial and said it freed him to enter a creative period (NeXT, Pixar), and NeXT tech later returned to Apple.
- Habits: For 33 years asked himself in the mirror each morning whether he would want to do today's tasks if it were his last day; if "no" too many days in a row, he changed something (NPR transcript of the speech).
- Mentors/accountability: Bill Campbell coached him at Apple and visited almost daily during his illness (Fast Company, CNBC via Trillion Dollar Coach coverage).
- Avoidance: not found.
- Setbacks: reframed firing as release; kept building.
- Sources: https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says ; https://www.npr.org/transcripts/141122856 ; https://www.fastcompany.com/90331367/the-coach-behind-jobs-brin-and-sandberg-had-a-secret-weapon-love ; Confidence: high (speech is primary), medium for Campbell link.

## Jeff Bezos (yes/partly; e-commerce; scale not sourced)
- Early: Left a senior VP role at D.E. Shaw in 1994 at about 30 using a "regret minimization" test (imagine being 80) (secondary sources; exact quote unverified).
- Habits: Own shareholder letter: decide with about 70% of the information wanted; correct bad decisions quickly; judge process but use bad outcomes to improve the process. Meetings start with silent reading of narrative memos instead of PowerPoint (2018 letter; reading time varies by source, 15-30 min).
- Mentors/accountability: not found.
- Avoidance: letter warns against "Day 2" process-over-outcome behaviour.
- Setbacks: expects failed experiments as price of invention (letter).
- Sources: https://www.cnbc.com/2017/04/12/amazon-jeff-bezos-2017-shareholder-letter.html (read in full) ; https://www.inc.com/justin-bariso/amazon-jeff-bezos-powerpoint-meetings-how-to-think.html ; https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2016/10/the-jeff-bezos-regret-minimization-framework/ ; Confidence: high for letter, medium for rest.

## PATTERNS ACROSS THE COHORT

N = 37 entries (38 people). Counts below are "documented in my sources", not "true for the person". Because my search queries asked about failure and habits, absence often means "I did not find it". The counts are a lower bound and are biased toward the topics I searched.

Strongest-supported first:

1. Survived a major documented setback, rejection, forced exit or near-death and kept going: 23/37 (Jobs, Musk, Zuckerberg, Page/Brin, Huang, Hoffman, Horowitz, Thiel, Chesky, Butterfield, Kalanick, Hastings, Houston, Wolfe Herd, Luckey, Knight, Schultz, Blakely, Cuban, Bloomberg, Oprah, Ellison, Wozniak). Many of these came from being pushed out or rejected and starting again within months, not from careful planning.
2. Built with a co-founder or close partner rather than alone: 14/37 (Gates, Jobs, Page/Brin, Collison, Knight, Chesky, Houston, Ek, Hastings, Armstrong, Spiegel, Ellison, Bloomberg, Kalanick). Houston and Armstrong were explicitly told by YC to find a co-founder.
3. Openly admitted or reframed mistakes as learning: 13/37 (Jobs, Bezos, Zuckerberg, Hoffman, Hastings, Blakely, Thiel, Ellison, Gates, Nadella, Horowitz, Kalanick, Huang).
4. Had a named coach, mentor, or accountability structure: 11/37 (Jobs, Page/Brin, Schmidt, Chesky, Houston, Collison, Hoffman, Armstrong, Altman, Knight, Butterfield via investors). 7 of these trace to just two sources, Paul Graham/YC or Bill Campbell: that is clustering, not 11 independent findings.
5. Explicit personal routine, review ritual, or reading discipline: 7/37 (Jobs mirror question, Dorsey, Zuckerberg annual challenges, Moskovitz meditation, Gates and Andreessen reading, Blakely family failure ritual).
6. Moved fast / did unscalable things / shipped early: 7/37 (Chesky, Collison, Hoffman, Bezos, Horowitz, Altman, Houston demo video).
7. Pivoted after a setback instead of quitting: 7/37 (Butterfield, Lutke, Horowitz, Hastings, Wolfe Herd, Bloomberg, Kalanick).
8. Extreme work volume documented: 5/37 (Gates, Musk, Dorsey, Cuban, Huang). Counter-evidence: Gates later regretted it; Moskovitz and Dorsey emphasise recovery practices.

Not found (important gap): documented, specific admissions of avoidance or comfort-zone behaviour were rare in my sources (Schmidt initially resisting coaching, Dell/Cuban refusing tasks they were assigned, Gates's later regret about intensity, Houston's botched unannounced visit). I found no reliable source on how any of them handled day-to-day discipline lapses. The "avoidance" field is therefore mostly empty; do not infer a pattern from it.

### Contradictory findings
- Hours: Gates, Musk, Cuban, Dorsey (80-hour week report) worked extremely hard; Gates later said that intensity was not always appropriate; Moskovitz and the later Dorsey built recovery/meditation into the system. No source here shows which works better.
- Process: Horowitz says a wartime CEO violates protocol and moves fast; Hastings dropped rules; Bezos built memos and fixed meeting format; Huang uses no one-on-ones. Different companies, opposite structures.
- Coaching: Page and Schmidt were coached 1:1 by Campbell; Huang runs without one-on-ones. Both lines are documented.
- Pain: Huang says he wishes students "pain and suffering" and (2026 report) would not restart if he knew; Moskovitz stresses equanimity. Ek and Thiel called ideas "terrible" or rejection "devastating", yet later recast it as fortunate.
- Early exit: Page/Brin tried to sell cheap and failed (lucky); Spiegel reportedly declined $3B and it worked. Hindsight decides.
- Formal path: Dell, Ellison, Kalanick, Gates left school; Thiel and Bezos followed conventional high-status careers first.

### Survivorship bias and source quality
- Every person is a winner selected on outcome. The many who also worked 100 hours, had mentors and failed are not in the sample, so none of the counts above show cause.
- Many "failures" are told in the founders' own redemptive retellings (Jobs speech, Blakely, Thiel, Hoffman, Hastings), which tend to smooth the story; Excite CEO's account of the Google offer contradicts the popular version.
- Several people had real advantages not researched here (Gates family background; Schultz's origin story is contested; Mark Cuban's and Altman's exits were modest/lucky by their own words).
- Secondhand/listicle-type sources: Business Insider (and Indian mirrors), Inc., Fortune recaps, Shortform, Medium, Stacksync, Rework, Celebrity Net Worth, Taskade, LinkedIn posts, Wikipedia mirrors, Perlego/Scribd book pages. Book claims (Shoe Dog, Hard Thing, Trillion Dollar Coach, No Rules Rules) were seen through summaries, not read.
- Conflicting facts in sources: Musk's 2008 Falcon 1 result; Excite offer price ($250K-$1M); Chesky rejection counts; Kalanick's Red Swoosh sale price ($15-23M); Cuban's MicroSolutions price ($3M vs $6M); Huang's 55 vs 60 direct reports.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR A MENTOR

Context: a 21-year-old CS student with a remote infra job and his own product. These are my inferences from the evidence above, labelled with strength.

1. Make setback recovery the core metric, not streaks. 23/37 documented a major setback and continued; what repeated was restart speed (Oprah onto a new show within about a year; Bloomberg founding a firm after being let go; Butterfield pivoting within months). The mentor should respond to a missed week by asking "what is the smallest restart today" rather than shaming. Evidence: moderate, but the pattern is real; survivorship caveat applies.
2. Push him to find a co-founder or a named accountability partner. 14/37 had a partner; YC told Houston and Armstrong to find one. For a solo student, the mentor should treat "find one person who sees my weekly numbers" as a goal. Evidence: moderate.
3. Build a weekly "what did I fail at / try that might fail" check-in. Blakely's family ritual and Zuckerberg/Jobs-style self-review are the best documented routines (Blakely medium-high; Jobs primary source). Ask: one thing you attempted that could have failed, one rejection or cold contact. Evidence: moderate; these are founders' own accounts.
4. Prompt "do the unscalable, manual version first" and require shipped output. Chesky (cereal boxes, going to users), Collison (installing for users), Houston (demo video), Hoffman (embarrassing v1). For his product: demand a user contact count each week, not features. Evidence: moderate, same source cluster (Graham/YC).
5. Add a daily kill-or-continue question. Jobs's mirror question (primary source) is a cheap, documented self-audit: if the answer is "no" repeatedly, change something. Use it as the daily message, not a mood check. Evidence: single strong anecdote.
6. Do not copy the "100-hour week" myth; set a ceiling and a recovery routine. Only 5/37 documented extreme hours, and Gates later said it was not always appropriate; Moskovitz/Dorsey built recovery practices. With a full-time remote job plus a product, burnout is the likely failure; the mentor should track sleep/rest as a lead indicator. Evidence: mixed; caution.
7. Give him rejection reps on a schedule. Schultz (217 of 242 said no), Chesky, Blakely, Houston: repeated rejection was routine. Set a weekly number of asks/outreach (users, clients, mentors) and log outcomes; rejection counts as completion. Evidence: moderate.
8. Make the mentor ask for decisions with an explicit deadline and a reversibility label. Bezos's 70% rule and fast correction (primary letter) fit a 21-year-old who may over-plan. Ask "reversible or not, and what is the decision date". Evidence: primary for the idea; no evidence it works for students.
9. Help him get an external adviser, not just a bot. 11/37 had named mentors, and Campbell/Graham alone account for 7. The mentor should push him to apply for a program (YC-style), find a senior infra engineer to review his plan monthly, and report back. Evidence: moderate, clustered.
10. Run an explicit "avoidance log". The evidence on avoidance is thin (a gap), so do not assume the usual avoidance suspects: have him record the one task he postponed each day and the reason, then review weekly. This is a hypothesis test on his own data, not a founder pattern. Evidence: none directly; flagged as inference.
11. Keep honesty from the mentor: public admission of mistakes appears in 13/37 (Zuckerberg's Beacon apology, Hastings's reversal, Bezos's letter). The mentor should ask for short written post-mortems of missed commitments within 24 hours, and review them weekly, using the Bezos "improve the process, not just the result" idea. Evidence: moderate.
12. Treat all of this as hypotheses. The corpus is winners only, mostly self-told, partly aggregator-sourced. The mentor should measure his own weekly evidence (commitments hit, user contacts, rejections taken, sleep) and let his data override these founder stories.
