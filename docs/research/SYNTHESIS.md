# Research synthesis for the mentor (2026-11-20)

## What was actually done (honest numbers)
- 157 people searched across four cohorts: US founders 38 (A), investors 36 (B), global and Indian founders 39 (C), modern
  operators and coaches 44 (D). Not "thousands" and not "hundreds of deeply studied": it is 157 names, with usable
  behaviour-level evidence for roughly 50-plus of them (A about 15, B about 12, D about 25, C not reported separately).
- Cohort E: the behaviour-change and coaching science (10 topics, about 8 sources read directly, the rest tagged by
  confidence). Cohort F: about 55-60 unique comfort-zone, rejection and mentor-relationship cases (36 rows verified by reading
  the page, 24 from search summaries, 26 recalled and unchecked, 4 myths).
- Method limits: web search summaries, few full pages read, many secondhand sources (Business Insider, Inc., Wikipedia, Indian
  business press, course-selling creators). Habits and avoidance were rarely documented. Everything below is a hypothesis to test
  on Daksh's own data, not a recipe. Files: docs/research/cohort-a ... cohort-f.

## What the evidence supports (strong enough to build on)
1. If-then plans work (implementation intentions, d about 0.65 across 94 tests). Every commitment = cue + place + first action.
2. Setbacks are universal and restart speed is what repeats: 23 of 37 US founders, 11 of 36 investors, 9 of 38 global founders
   had a documented early failure and kept going. Fast restart, not streaks, is the right metric.
3. What breaks "hiding in building" is contact with real people: 6 of 11 documented cases (Airbnb, Stripe, Wufoo, Pinterest).
   After a "no", the verified cases changed something (pitch, channel, product); none just repeated the same ask.
4. Mentors that worked (Bill Campbell, verified) asked questions, gave candid feedback fast and privately, paired with care,
   and let the mentee decide. Harshness mostly appears in survivor stories and cost a lot when public or status-based.
5. Feedback aimed at the person backfires (Kluger and DeNisi: feedback lowered performance in over a third of cases). Shame
   feeds hiding, which is exactly his failure mode.
6. Penalties work only when self-chosen in advance, small and fixed (SEED: about 81% more savings but only 28% opted in).
   Doubling quotas or growing penalties after a miss triggers the "what-the-hell" relapse. No trial tests AI-imposed penalties.
7. Do the job first: about half of global founders (about 20 of 38) worked in an employer or trade first. No one supports "$1M by 25"
   as typical. Realistic indie timelines are 3 to 5 years, with most projects failing (Levels: about 4 of 70+ projects paid).
8. Investors' written mistakes practices (Buffett's annual list, Munger/Pabrai checklists, Bridgewater issue log) exist, but no
   investor was documented keeping a decision journal. A daily commit-and-review log is therefore an inference, flagged as such.
9. A 2026 preprint (n = 517) found an AI goal coach beat no support (d = 0.33), mediated by felt accountability; the main risk is
   sycophancy, so completion needs evidence.

## What is weak or myth (do not say it to him as fact)
Wealth-habit books and "rich habits" lists; "21 days to form a habit" (real mean about 66 days, range 18-254); grit slogans;
Sanders's "1,009 rejections" and Disney's "302 banks"; 100-hour-week hero stories (documented for only 5 of 37 US founders);
the Blockbuster $50M story; most rejection counts are self-reported. Where a number is used with him it must be checked first.

## Findings that overrode the first design draft
| First draft said | Evidence says | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Double the missed output next day (penalty C3) | Escalating or doubling after a miss causes relapse | Removed. After a miss: a minimal recovery action today |
| Hard mode names him ("you're hiding") | Self-directed feedback hurts; shame feeds hiding | Hard = firm about behaviour and the agreed rule, never labels him |
| Five invented personal stories for the persona | Stories only with named, checkable sources | Stories now only about real, checkable people |
| Penalties imposed by the bot after week 3 | Only self-chosen, small, fixed, in advance, renegotiable in calm | He authors the menu; bot never adds or grows |
| Mentor is a bot alone | Real accountability needs felt accountability and a human in the loop | Bot pushes him to name one human reviewer |
| (not in draft) | Early-warning signs (sleep, silence, self-criticism) mean stop pushing | Added a stop-pushing protocol |
